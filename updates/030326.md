PRINCE SHELL
I happen to stop in at the Musicians Union (across from Coronado Cafe at 12th Street & Oak) and met Cindy Baker, the Union President. I had no appointment, and she spent an hour with me.
I learned a great deal from Cindy. I told her about the Easter Parade & Matzo Ball and honoring Estelle’s Bistro, and a miracle happened.

Every weekday evening at Estelle’s Bistro, an unassuming, humble man named Prince Shell would sit at the piano and play tunes non-stop, with a quiet peaceful gracefulness. The show tunes from eras past rolled one into another without a breath. Prince never performed for applause, he played to fill the air with music, a rich soft backdrop. Prince would have been the last person to reveal anything about his past, his inspiring musical life.  He mentored young jazz musicians for decades. His charts were used in recordings that came out during the Civil Rights Movement; he worked with Jesse Jackson.
Prince was zen.

We are excited to announce that
The Prince Shell 10-Piece Orchestra
Conducted by Ted Goddard
Will perform in the Abbey ballroom after the Parade!
Cindy Baker connected us to this Prince Shell legacy orchestra.
Additionally, the Union is paying half the fees for the musicians from the MPTF: the Music Performance Trust Fund.

Ted Goddard is rousting the musicians for an exciting performance in the Abbey Ballroom after the Parade.
